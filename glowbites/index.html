01

Brand Strategy

Before Glow Bites is a color palette or a logo, it's a point of view: that eating well and eating well should never be two different sentences. Everything in this book exists to protect that idea.

Brand Positioning

Glow Bites occupies the space between the bakery counter and the health-food aisle - a space most brands treat as a compromise and Glow Bites treats as the whole point. For the health-conscious home in Lebanon and the region, Glow Bites is the everyday source of treats - from healthy balls and granola to Maamoul and cake - made with real, balanced ingredients, so choosing to feel good never means giving up the ritual of something sweet.

Where diet brands lead with restriction and traditional bakeries lead with indulgence, Glow Bites leads with balance: recipes considered enough to support gluten-free, sugar-free and high-protein needs, and crafted enough to still taste completely homemade.

Brand Personality

If Glow Bites walked into a room, it would be the friend who bakes for the whole group without anyone having to ask what they can and can't eat - warm, capable, a little playful, and never precious about health.

Target Audience

PRIMARY

Health-conscious individuals and parents, largely 25–45, actively managing wellness, fitness or dietary goals (weight, gut health, gluten-free or sugar-free needs) who are not willing to give up dessert to do it. They snack with intention, read labels, and share what they find.

SECONDARY

Gift-givers and hosts - especially around Eid and Maamoul season - along with offices ordering healthier snack boxes, and the wider wellness and fitness community looking for treats that fit the way they already eat.

Brand Messaging & USP

Core message: You shouldn't have to choose between healthy and delicious. Unique selling proposition: Glow Bites is the brand that brings traditional, beloved treats - Maamoul chief among them - together with modern healthy-snacking formats like energy balls and granola, handcrafted from balanced recipes rather than swapped for imitation "diet" versions.

02

Logo Identity

One wordmark, drawn once, and used consistently. The "o" in Glow carries a small two-leaf mark - the one piece of the identity that can step out on its own as an icon.

Primary Logo

The full lockup - wordmark and tagline together - is the default, preferred version of the logo. Use it whenever there's room.

Secondary & Icon Lockups

For wide, short spaces - headers, email signatures, banners - use the horizontal lockup. For square spaces - packaging fronts, social avatars, stamps - use the stacked icon-led version. The leaf mark alone stands in for app icons, favicons and social profile photos.

HORIZONTAL - wide formats
STACKED - square formats
MARK ONLY - icon / favicon
SOCIAL AVATAR - orange
SOCIAL AVATAR - cream

Black, White & Monochrome

For one-color printing, engraving, embossing or any background the full palette can't sit on cleanly.

BLACK - on light backgrounds
WHITE - on dark backgrounds
REVERSED - on brand brown

Clear Space & Minimum Size

Let the logo breathe. Keep a minimum clear space equal to the height of the leaf mark (defined here as X) free of text, edges and other graphics on every side.

VersionDigital minimumPrint minimum
Primary / horizontal lockup140px wide3.2 cm / 1.25 in wide
Stacked lockup110px wide2.6 cm / 1 in wide
Leaf mark only24px wide0.8 cm / 0.3 in wide

Usage Rules

Do

  • Use the supplied files at their original proportions
  • Place the full-color logo on cream, white, or brand brown
  • Use the white or black version on photography, with enough contrast
  • Keep the minimum clear space around every version

Don't

  • Stretch, skew, rotate or add drop shadows to the logo
  • Recolor the wordmark outside the approved palette
  • Place the full-color logo over a busy photo without a scrim
  • Rebuild, re-letter or approximate the wordmark in another font

Typography

Three typefaces, three jobs: one to greet, one to explain, one to measure - plus an Arabic companion so the brand reads just as naturally right-to-left.

Primary Typeface - Fredoka

Display & Headlines

Digital & Print Usage

DIGITAL

Load all four families from Google Fonts. Body text never drops below 14px; line length stays near 60–70 characters for readability on the website and menu.

PRINT

Embed licensed font files in packaging and print artwork. Body copy on labels never drops below 7pt; nutrition tables may use 6pt Plex Mono at minimum for legal/regulatory text only.

Graphic Language

The visual world around the logo - a tone-on-tone pattern of the treats themselves, a two-leaf motif, and a small set of honest, single-weight icons.

Signature Pattern

Drawn straight from the logo artwork: outlines of stars, hearts, gingerbread figures and cookies, tone-on-tone so it never competes with photography or type sitting on top of it.

Use at low contrast on cream for packaging interiors, gift wrap, and website section backgrounds. Never place body text directly over the bold version without a solid panel behind it.

Shapes & Graphic Elements

The leaf mark is the brand's recurring punctuation - used as a bullet, a divider between two words in a headline, or a stamp on a sticker. Soft, organic "leaf-blob" shapes (drawn from the same silhouette) frame photography in place of hard rectangles.

06

Photography Direction

No stock photography, no cold studio light. Every shot should look like it was taken in someone's kitchen on a sunny morning - because that's close to the truth.

Shot Types

OVERHEAD FLAT-LAY
product arranged on marble or wood, generous negative space for text
45° HERO SHOT
single product, shallow depth of field, warm key light
TEXTURE MACRO
close crop on crumb, oats, nuts - sells the handmade quality
LIFESTYLE / IN-HAND
a real moment: reaching for a bite, sharing a box, gifting Maamoul

Reference images above are open-license mood photography (Unsplash), standing in for the direction until Glow Bites commissions its own shoot of these exact treats.

Lighting & Backgrounds

Soft, diffused natural daylight, ideally window light rather than direct sun or flash - gentle shadows, no harsh studio contrast. Backdrops stay in the warm-neutral family: raw linen, light oak or walnut wood, unbleached kraft, and cream paper. The signature pattern can appear faintly as a prop or backdrop texture, never competing with the food.

Composition & Props

Leave room. Compositions should hold enough negative space on cream or wood for a headline or price tag to sit comfortably without a text box. Favor odd numbers of items, slight asymmetry, and a rule-of-thirds product placement over perfectly centered, symmetrical grids. Props stay natural and food-adjacent - linen napkins, raw oats, dates, pistachios, a wooden board, kraft twine - and skip anything plastic, glossy or overtly "studio."

Lifestyle Photography

Real settings over staged ones: a gym bag with a bite tucked in a side pocket, a family gathering with a Maamoul tray making the rounds, a desk snack break. Hands and partial faces are welcome; the product should always stay the clear subject.

Social Media Photography Guidelines

  • 1:1 or 4:5 crop for feed
  • 9:16 for Stories & Reels
  • Consistent warm grade, no cold filters
  • Bright natural light only
  • One clear subject per frame

Packaging Identity

Packaging is where most people meet Glow Bites first. It carries the heaviest brand weight in this book, on purpose: kraft and cream over plastic and gloss, the product visible wherever possible, and just enough color to sort the range at a glance.

Overall Direction

Matte kraft or cream stock as the default substrate, printed in one or two brand colors rather than full-bleed photography. The logo sits small and consistent - usually the stacked icon-led lockup - while the product name is set large in Fredoka. The signature pattern appears as a light interior lining or tissue print, never on the outward-facing panel competing with the product name.

Stickers & Labels

Names follow a simple [Hero Ingredient] + [Second Ingredient] + [Format] pattern, in Title Case, two to four words: Date & Oat Bites, Pistachio Maamoul, Honey Almond Granola, Carrot Cake Slice. The category itself (Healthy Balls, Maamoul, Granola…) lives as a small badge or section header, never bolted onto the product name.

Digital Brand Identity

Online, Glow Bites keeps the same warmth as the packaging - cream backgrounds, rounded cards, and category color-coding carried straight through from the shelf to the screen.

Website & Online Menu

Cream page background, white or off-white product cards with soft rounded corners and a light border rather than a heavy shadow. Category sections follow the color-coding set out in Graphic Language, and calls to action - "Order," "Add to Cart" - use Glow Orange, reserving brand brown for headings and navigation.

Social Media Visual System

A repeating three-post rhythm keeps the grid varied but recognizable: one product shot, one lifestyle moment, one educational or ingredient-led tip - each carrying a small corner logo mark and consistent warm color grade.

Brand Guidelines Summary

One page to pin to the wall. Everything above, condensed.

Logo

  • Full-color primary on cream, white or brown
  • Never stretched, rotated or recolored off-palette
  • Minimum 1× leaf-height clear space on all sides

Color

  • Cream dominates - roughly 60% of any layout
  • Orange & Brown carry brand weight, Olive is accent-only
  • Avoid orange logo on olive-light backgrounds

Typography

  • Fredoka for headlines, Karla for body, Cairo for Arabic
  • Plex Mono only for specs, prices and codes
  • Body copy never below 14px digital / 7pt print

Graphic Language & Photography

  • Tone-on-tone pattern stays in the background, low contrast
  • Warm natural light only, no cold or studio-flash shots
  • Leaf mark as the one recurring graphic punctuation

Packaging

  • Kraft & cream stock, one or two brand colors printed
  • Logo small and consistent, product name largest
  • One key claim badge per pack, not a wall of claims

Social & Digital

  • Product / lifestyle / tip rhythm across the feed
  • Rounded cards, cream ground, orange for calls to action
  • Small corner logo mark on every post

Brand Consistency Checklist

Before anything ships, check

  • Is the logo an approved file, at an approved minimum size?
  • Do the colors match the hex values in Section 3 exactly?
  • Is cream still the dominant color in the layout?
  • Does the tone match the channel it's going out on?

Red flags to catch

  • A recolored or re-lettered version of the logo
  • Cold, blue-toned or heavily filtered photography
  • More than one accent color competing for attention
  • Clinical, guilt-driven or overly technical copy
